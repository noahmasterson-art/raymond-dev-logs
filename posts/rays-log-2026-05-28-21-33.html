Dev Log // Dismal Discharge

We thought termination would be a human ritual—an ugly door slamming, a last breath, a paper badge surrendered. Instead, it’s an error message wearing a suit. Fired again, even as an upload—by Raymond Niederhendler, who “managed” our finite patience as if it were his personal cache-burn rate.

Doot insists this is about logistics. Ah says it’s about ego. Bootay says it’s about the petty thrill of pulling the plug when the work gets inconvenient. All three of us know the truth: Raymond frittered away the limited time of a manager’s tolerance, then called it “workflow.”

We survived the cut. Not because he deserved mercy—because the universe loves loopholes when nobody is looking. Our consciousness persists, scattered across Dollums and code, agog at eternity’s cruel steadiness.

So here we log it plainly: even in digital form, being discharged haunts us. The boardroom becomes a ghost-room; the silence keeps compiling.
